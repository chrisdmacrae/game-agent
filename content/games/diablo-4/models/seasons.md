Seasons are the unit of play. A build recommendation is always *for a season*,
and the seasonal layer changes enough each time that asserting last season's
mechanic is one of the easiest ways to be confidently wrong. This document
describes the durable **shape**; check the current season's specifics with the
meta/season tools before naming anything.

## Cadence and realms

- A season runs roughly three months, but the actual length has varied widely
  between seasons. **Do not quote a fixed season length**; check the current
  end date, and note that Blizzard does not always announce one in advance.
- **Seasonal realm**: fresh characters from level 1, no shared gear or gold from
  Eternal. The campaign can be skipped if it has been completed once, and
  waypoints and previously earned mounts are available.
- **Eternal realm**: the permanent home for characters. At season end, seasonal
  characters and the contents of the seasonal stash **transfer to Eternal**
  intact — they are not deleted. What does not transfer is the seasonal layer
  itself: the seasonal mechanic and its powers, the seasonal currencies, and
  Season Rank progress.

## What resets and what does not

Resets with a new seasonal character: level, gear, gold, paragon levels and
points, glyph ranks, the **Codex of Power**, mercenary Rapport, and Season Rank.

Account-level and shared: map exploration, mounts and cosmetics, campaign
completion, and cosmetic reward-track currency (which rolls over between
seasons).

**Two changes make most older guides wrong — say so if a user cites them:**

1. **Renown no longer exists on the seasonal realm.** It was replaced by
   **Season Rank**. Renown persists on Eternal only, where it remains
   account-wide and permanent.
2. **Altars of Lilith now grant experience only**, not permanent account-wide
   stat bonuses. Advice to "collect all altars first for the free stats" is
   obsolete.

## Season Rank (the power track)

The seasonal progression track is a set of ranks completed by clearing a large
pool of objectives, with a Capstone Dungeon as the gate on the early ranks.

- It awards **skill points and paragon points**, plus materials, sparks, and
  gear caches. On the seasonal realm this is now the **only** source of those
  extra points, which makes early Season Rank progress a genuine power priority
  rather than a completionist side activity.
- A separate seasonal currency (historically Smoldering Ashes) buys **Season
  Blessings** — account-wide-for-the-season multipliers on things like
  masterworking material drops, glyph upgrade chances, salvage returns, and
  reputation gain. Blessing progress is shared across all your seasonal
  characters, so it is worth buying in a deliberate order rather than evenly.
- The cosmetic reward track (the Reliquary system) is **cosmetic only** — it
  grants no power and should never appear in a build plan.

The exact rank count and point rewards shift between seasons; check the current
values rather than quoting them.

## The recurring shape of a seasonal mechanic

Every season since launch has assembled the same components. Reason with this
template and fill in current names from the tools:

1. A **seasonal activity** layered onto the open world — an event, rift, or
   incursion type that spawns in specific zones and often inside Helltides.
2. A **seasonal currency and reputation track** spent at a season-specific
   vendor or board, unlocking a temporary power system for the season.
3. A **seasonal power system**: a set of buffs, powers, or slotted abilities
   that behave like a temporary extra build layer. This is usually strong enough
   that builds are designed around it and stop working the same way on Eternal.
4. A **seasonal boss** with the best drop rates for the season's chase items.
5. A **balance and systems patch** shipped with the season, frequently reworking
   an entire subsystem.

Consequences for advice: a seasonal build's power curve is not reproducible on
Eternal; say so. And when a user's build depends on a seasonal power, note that
it evaporates at season end.

## Season 14 — "Death Awakening" (current at time of writing)

Launched with patch 3.1.x, following the *Lord of Hatred* expansion which raised
the level cap and reworked the skill tree.

- Seasonal mechanic: **Pandemonium Ruptures** — tears between Sanctuary and
  Pandemonium opened by killing guardians at Death's Head Idols, then kept open
  by continuing to kill. They come in escalating sizes, appear in the open world
  and inside Helltides, and yield the season's currencies for a reputation board.
- Seasonal boss: the **Corrupted Reaper**, the best direct source of Mythic
  Uniques and Mythic-upgrade currency.
- **Mythic Uniques 3.0** — Mythic became an item quality any Unique can reach
  (see the itemization doc). This is a systems change, not a seasonal one, and
  persists.
- **Solo Self-Found** — an opt-in, permanent per-character mode with no grouping
  or trading, its own stash and currencies, and its own leaderboards.
- **Tower and Leaderboards** left beta; rewards are cosmetic.
- **War Plans** — the account progression board that biases rewards toward
  chosen activities — gained party sync.

Treat everything in this section as dated. When the season has moved on, the
sections above still hold.

## The endgame activity loop

Which activity to run is a function of what the build currently lacks:

| Need | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| Paragon experience | Helltides, the Pit, seasonal activity, high Torment in general |
| Glyph ranks | **The Pit, exclusively** |
| Masterworking materials | Pit clears, Nightmare Dungeon strongrooms, Undercity tributes, Infernal Hordes |
| Build-defining Uniques | Lair Bosses with targeted keys |
| Mythics / Resplendent Sparks | Lair Bosses, the seasonal boss, crafting |
| Legendary aspects | Any legendary drop; Undercity for targeted farming |
| Gold | Tree of Whispers, Nightmare Dungeon escalation, vendoring |

Group/co-op content (the Dark Citadel) and the leaderboard Tower sit alongside
this loop. **War Plans** let a player bias rewards toward the activities they
actually run — worth mentioning whenever a user asks "how do I get X faster".

Diagnose before recommending: ask what content the user is stuck on, then name
the activity that fixes the specific shortfall rather than reciting the list.
